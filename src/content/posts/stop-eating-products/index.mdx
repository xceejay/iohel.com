Most people don't eat food anymore.
They eat products.

Boxes, packets, drinks that never go bad. "Snacks" with a shelf life longer than some countries. Whole food is the exception; ultra-processed is the default.

The usual argument:

> "Real food is expensive. Processed food is cheap."

Short term, that's true.
Long term, it flips.

You either pay for real food now, or you pay for drugs, doctors, and lost years later. There is no version where you eat garbage for 40 years and get away with it.

## The cost of "cheap" food

Ultra-processed food isn't just "more calories." It's:

- Seed oils pushed way beyond sane use
- Sugar in everything
- Starches and gums for texture
- Additives no normal kitchen has
- Engineered to be inhaled, not eaten

That combo does a few things reliably:

- Wrecks blood sugar control
- Confuses hunger signals (you're full and starving at the same time)
- Drops food quality while raising calorie density

You end up eating more, feeling worse, and blaming your body instead of the inputs.

### What studies tend to find

Across large population studies in different countries, people who get a big chunk of their calories from ultra-processed foods tend to have:

- Higher rates of obesity and type 2 diabetes
- More cardiovascular disease
- Higher overall mortality

These are associations, not perfect proof of cause, but when you see the same pattern repeat across millions of people and different cultures, you take it seriously.

## The food-drug loop

Then you "need":

- Blood pressure meds
- Cholesterol drugs
- Blood sugar meds
- Antacids
- Sleep pills

The same system that sold you "cheap" food now sells you what you need to live with the damage.

### What studies tend to find

- Diets high in ultra-processed foods are linked to higher use of multiple prescription drugs at the same time in older adults.
- When people switch to a minimally processed, higher-protein, higher-fibre diet in controlled trials, they often spontaneously eat less and see better blood sugar and blood pressure without touching anything else.

Change the inputs and the "need" for some of the meds drops.

## Follow the ownership, not the logo

This isn't about one villain brand.
It's about who owns what.

Big asset managers - BlackRock, Vanguard, State Street, and company - sit on the cap table of:

- Global food and beverage companies
- Household and personal care brands
- Pharma and biotech
- "Wellness" and supplement companies

Different logos on shelves.
Same major shareholders behind the scenes.

So you get:

- Products that dysregulate hormones and metabolism
- Products to "fix" your skin, house, and mood
- Drugs to manage the chronic conditions that follow

You don't need a conspiracy thread. Incentives are enough. When the same capital benefits from both the problem and the treatment, your long-term health isn't the priority.

You won't find a randomized trial for that. You find it in ownership graphs and profit reports.

## Your body is already under load

Even if you never touched junk food, your body still deals with:

- Air pollution
- Microplastics
- Sitting too long
- Screens and blue light late at night
- Work and money stress
- A news cycle that never shuts up

You can't control all of that.

So what you *can* control matters more:

- What you eat
- When you sleep
- How often you move
- How wound up you let yourself stay

Food is one of the biggest levers you still own.

### What studies tend to find

- Sleep restriction on its own (without diet change) makes people more insulin-resistant and hungrier the next day.
- Chronic psychosocial stress is linked to higher visceral fat and worse metabolic markers, even at similar body weight.
- Adding regular physical activity improves blood sugar and blood pressure *even when* body weight barely changes.

Your body carries a lot already. Food, sleep, and movement change how heavy that load feels.

## Whole food is "boring" on purpose

Real food is simple:

- Meat, fish, eggs
- Vegetables, fruit
- Potatoes, rice, oats
- Olive oil, butter
- Nuts, seeds, beans if they agree with you

No ingredient list. No mascot. No buzzwords.

People call it "boring".
Against engineered hyper-flavour, sure.

Give your taste buds two to four weeks away from constant sugar and flavourings and you start tasting food again. Tomatoes are sweet. Good eggs have flavour. Olive oil has bite. Fruit tastes like dessert.

The diet industry sells complicated.
Your body runs on boring.

### What studies tend to find

Controlled feeding studies that swap ultra-processed meals for minimally processed ones (same calories, same macros) often show:

- People eat fewer calories when allowed to eat as much as they want
- They report feeling more satisfied
- They lose weight or improve risk markers without "trying"

Same calories on paper.
Different behaviour and outcomes in reality.

## "But real food is expensive"

On paper:

- Soda + instant noodles + processed meat: cheap
- Quality meat + vegetables + fruit: expensive

Zoom out:

- The cheap meal nudges you toward chronic issues you'll "manage" for decades
- The better meal reduces that risk and keeps you functioning

Money you "save" on food leaks out later as:

- Monthly meds
- More sick days, less energy
- Stuff you buy to feel better because you feel like trash

Most people don't overspend on meat and vegetables.
They overspend on:

- Constant snacks
- Deliveries and takeout
- Drinks (alcohol, sugary coffee, energy drinks)

Cut that, cook more, and "expensive" food often fits the same budget.

## Hormones aren't optional

Your body runs on signals:

- Insulin: what to do with energy
- Leptin/ghrelin: hunger and satiety
- Cortisol: stress response
- Sex hormones: mood, muscle, fat storage

Ultra-processed food, chronic stress, and sleep debt scramble those signals. Then you add endocrine disruptors from:

- Plastics and packaging
- Fragrances and personal care products
- Certain household chemicals

You can't dodge all of it. You live in 2026.

### What studies tend to find

- Higher exposure to certain plasticizers and "forever chemicals" consistently shows up alongside higher risk of obesity, insulin resistance, and fertility issues in population data.
- Eating more whole foods and fewer packaged products reduces the amount of these chemicals measured in urine and blood within days to weeks in intervention studies.

You can't avoid every hit.
You *can* stop eating and drinking out of it all day.

So you control what you can:

- Eat things that grew or lived, not powder that got extruded
- Sleep at sane hours (midnight isn't "early")
- Lower background stress (fewer alerts, less doom-scrolling)
- Keep caffeine and alcohol in check
- Skip unnecessary fake scents and plastics on your skin and in your food

Your hormones still have to work.
Don't bury them on purpose.

## This isn't about perfection

You're not going "pure". You're going to:

- Eat out
- Have cake
- Grab junk at an airport

That's fine.

The point:
Stop eating *like that* by default.

If most of your meals are:

- Cooked at home
- Built from whole ingredients
- Not drenched in sugar and weird oils

The occasional nonsense barely moves the needle.

If you live on ultra-processed food, no workout plan, supplement stack, or "hack" will bail you out.

## The simple version

If you want one rule:

> Eat food your great-grandparents would recognize, most of the time.

Then stack a few boring habits:

- Go to bed earlier than your phone wants you to
- Move daily (walks count)
- Don't let stress sit in your body all week
- Be suspicious of anything with a marketing budget bigger than its ingredient list

You're not going to escape every modern stressor.
You *can* stop making your body fight your food too.
